RTK and Differential GPS: Achieving Sub-Centimeter Velocity Metrology

Standard satellite GPS delivers 2 to 5 meters of positional accuracy. Discover how Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) carrier phase tracking and NTRIP reference base stations achieve sub-centimeter positioning and ultra-precise velocity ground truth.

1. The Limitations of Standard Pseudorange GPS

Standard commercial GNSS receivers—including the chip inside your smartphone used by our AeroSpeed Speedometer—compute position by measuring the time delay of pseudorandom digital noise codes (such as the GPS C/A code at 1.023 MHz).

Because electromagnetic radio signals travel at the speed of light ($c \approx 299,792,458 \text{ m/s}$), an error of just 10 nanoseconds in satellite clock synchronization or ionospheric transit time introduces a 3-meter position error.

For autonomous vehicle lane-level positioning, high-speed rail control, and certified automotive crash testing, 3-meter error is unacceptable. This demand created Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) positioning.

2. The Carrier Phase Tracking Breakthrough

Instead of aligning coarse digital code pulses (which have a wavelength of approximately 300 meters), RTK directly measures the physical carrier radio waves themselves:

  • GPS L1 Carrier Frequency: $1575.42 \text{ MHz}$ ($\lambda = \frac{c}{f} \approx 19.03 \text{ cm}$)
  • GPS L2 Carrier Frequency: $1227.60 \text{ MHz}$ ($\lambda \approx 24.42 \text{ cm}$)
  • GPS L5 / Galileo E5a: $1176.45 \text{ MHz}$ ($\lambda \approx 25.48 \text{ cm}$)

By measuring fractional phase shifts down to 1% of a carrier wave cycle, an RTK receiver can detect physical displacements of 1.9 millimeters!

Carrier Phase Observation Equation
\Phi = \frac{1}{\lambda} \left( \rho + c(\delta t_r - \delta t^s) - I + T \right) + N + \epsilon

Where $\Phi$ is measured carrier phase (cycles), $\rho$ is true geometric range, $N$ is the integer cycle ambiguity, $I$ is ionospheric delay, and $T$ is tropospheric delay.

3. How RTK Base Stations and NTRIP Work

The fundamental challenge with carrier phase tracking is the Integer Ambiguity Problem ($N$): the receiver knows the exact fraction of a 19-cm wave, but doesn't immediately know how many full wave cycles ($N$) exist between the satellite in medium Earth orbit (20,200 km altitude) and the vehicle.

To solve this, RTK pairs the vehicle receiver (the Rover) with a stationary reference station (the Base) installed at an exactly surveyed geodetic monument.

  1. Error Cancellation: Because the Base Station knows its precise physical coordinates to millimeter accuracy, it calculates the exact atmospheric distortion errors currently affecting all satellites in view.
  2. Real-Time Correction Stream (RTCM): The Base streams correction packets (using the RTCM 3.x standard) over cellular internet using NTRIP (Networked Transport of RTCM via Internet Protocol).
  3. Double-Differencing Algorithm: The vehicle's RTK computer subtracts the Base errors from its own measurements, mathematically canceling out satellite clock bias, satellite orbit drift, and 99% of atmospheric delays.

4. Velocity Metrology: Doppler vs RTK Phase Rates

How does RTK enhance vehicle speed calculations?

  • Standard Doppler Velocity: As detailed in our Doppler Velocity Physics Guide, consumer GPS calculates velocity from carrier frequency shift with an accuracy of $\pm 0.1 \text{ km/h}$.
  • RTK Time-Differenced Carrier Phase (TDCP): RTK samples carrier phase differences between consecutive epochs at 20 Hz to 100 Hz. This yields instantaneous 3D velocity vectors with an accuracy of $\pm 0.01 \text{ km/h}$ ($\pm 2.7 \text{ mm/s}$).

Industry Application

Automotive manufacturers use RTK multi-antenna setups to measure not only true vehicle speed, but also dynamic chassis slip angle ($\beta$), roll angle, and pitch during high-speed emergency lane change maneuvers (ISO 3888-2 elk test).

5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can smartphones use RTK?
Modern flagship phones with dual-frequency GNSS chips (like Broadcom BCM47765) can output raw carrier phase measurements (Android GNSS Raw Measurements API). When connected to a local NTRIP caster, phones can achieve 20–50 cm positioning under clear sky conditions.
What is an RTK "Fixed" vs "Float" state?
"RTK Fixed" occurs when the receiver has successfully resolved the integer cycle ambiguities ($N$), delivering 1-2 cm accuracy. "RTK Float" occurs when ambiguity is unresolved due to multipath reflections or poor satellite geometry, yielding 20-50 cm accuracy.
