Understanding Speed Cameras: Microwave Doppler Radar vs Laser LiDAR

Law enforcement traffic speed measurement relies on rigorous microwave physics and optical time-of-flight lasers. Explore the electromagnetic frequencies, cosine error geometry, and sensor mechanisms behind speed cameras.

1. The Evolution of Traffic Speed Measurement

Automated traffic speed enforcement and mobile police speed checks have evolved dramatically from early mechanical stopwatches and pneumatic road hoses into sophisticated electromagnetic and optical metrology systems.

Today, four primary technologies dominate global speed enforcement:

  1. Continuous Wave (CW) Microwave Doppler Radar
  2. Pulsed Infrared Laser LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)
  3. Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) Average Speed Systems
  4. In-Pavement Piezoelectric & Inductive Loop Sensors

2. Microwave Police Radar: Doppler Frequency Shift

Police radar units emit a continuous stream of electromagnetic radio waves at specific regulated microwave frequencies:

  • X-Band: $10.525\text{ GHz}$ (older stationary units)
  • K-Band: $24.150\text{ GHz}$ (common in automated roadside speed signs)
  • Ka-Band: $33.400 - 36.000\text{ GHz}$ (standard modern police radar guns)

When this microwave beam strikes a moving metallic vehicle, the reflected waves bounce back with an altered frequency due to the Doppler effect:

$$\Delta f = \frac{2 \cdot f_0 \cdot v}{c}$$

For a Ka-band radar gun transmitting at $f_0 = 34.7\text{ GHz}$, each $1\text{ km/h}$ of vehicle speed generates a Doppler shift of approximately $64.3\text{ Hz}$. A DSP mixer inside the radar gun isolates this audio frequency beat tone to compute vehicle speed in under 50 milliseconds.

3. The Cosine Angle Effect: Geometric Error

Police radar measures relative velocity directly along the line of sight between the radar antenna and the target vehicle. However, police cruisers and roadside speed camera gantries are positioned on the shoulder of the highway, offset by an angle $\theta$ from the vehicle's vector of travel.

The measured velocity $v_{\text{measured}}$ is:

$$v_{\text{measured}} = v_{\text{true}} \cdot \cos\theta$$

The Cosine Rule in Radar Metrology: Because $\cos\theta \le 1.0$ for all non-zero angles, the cosine effect always benefits the driver by causing the radar gun to read slightly lower than the vehicle's true road speed.

For example, if an officer targets a car traveling at an actual $100\text{ km/h}$ from an angle of $15^\circ$, the radar will read $100 \cdot \cos(15^\circ) = 96.6\text{ km/h}$.

4. Laser LiDAR Speed Measurement: Time-of-Flight Physics

Unlike microwave radar beams that spread widely (a radar beam is typically 60 meters wide at a distance of 300 meters), police laser guns (LiDAR) emit ultra-narrow pulses of coherent infrared light (typically $904\text{ nm}$ wavelength).

The beam divergence is only $3\text{ milliradians}$ (expanding to just $0.9\text{ meters}$ wide at $300\text{ meters}$), allowing officers to isolate a single vehicle in dense multi-lane traffic.

LiDAR does not use Doppler shift; instead, it fires hundreds of infrared light pulses in a fraction of a second (e.g., 200 pulses in 0.3 seconds) and calculates target distance for each pulse using the Time-of-Flight (ToF) formula:

$$d = \frac{c \cdot \Delta t_{\text{flight}}}{2}$$

By calculating the linear regression slope of distance changes across the pulse series ($\Delta d / \Delta t$), the LiDAR unit locks vehicle speed with an accuracy of $\pm 1.0\text{ km/h}$ in under $0.3\text{ seconds}$.

5. Average Speed Cameras (ANPR Section Control)

On modern motorways, point-in-time radar guns are increasingly replaced by Point-to-Point Average Speed Cameras (such as SPECS or Section Control).

An automated system uses high-resolution infrared cameras and Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) to capture your license plate and an atomic-clock synchronized timestamp at Point A, and again at Point B (e.g., 10 kilometers down the highway).

$$v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{D_{\text{certified}}}{T_B - T_A}$$

Because average speed cameras measure sustained velocity over long stretches, braking immediately before the camera gantry does not avoid a violation if the driver was speeding over the preceding distance.
