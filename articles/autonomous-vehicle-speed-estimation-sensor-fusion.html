How Autonomous Vehicles Estimate Velocity: Sensor Fusion of LiDAR, Radar, Cameras & IMU

Self-driving cars cannot rely on a single sensor to determine speed. Explore how multi-sensor fusion combining 3D LiDAR point clouds, 77GHz Doppler radar, visual odometry, and high-grade IMUs achieves millimeter-precision velocity estimation even in GPS-denied tunnels.

1. The Critical Need for Redundant Velocity Estimation

For Level 4 and Level 5 autonomous driving systems (such as Waymo, Cruise, and Baidu Apollo robotaxis), accurate vehicle velocity estimation is not just a dashboard display number—it is the life-critical state variable feeding trajectory planning, collision avoidance, and model predictive control (MPC) steering actuators.

If a single sensor fails or provides corrupted telemetry—such as GPS loss inside a subterranean tunnel or wheel spin on black ice—the vehicle must continue tracking its 3D velocity vector with absolute mathematical certainty.

2. The Sensor Fusion Architecture

Autonomous vehicle platforms combine five distinct sensing modalities:

Sensor Modality Physical Principle Update Rate Primary Advantage Failure Mode
Multi-Band RTK GNSS Satellite carrier wave phase & Doppler shift 10–20 Hz True geodetic ground truth ($< 0.01\text{ km/h}$ error) Tunnels, dense urban canyons, multipath
Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) MEMS/FOG 3-axis gyroscopes & accelerometers 100–1000 Hz Instantaneous response, zero latency Quadratic position drift over time ($t^2$)
77 GHz FMCW Radar Microwave Doppler frequency shifts from static scenery 20–50 Hz Direct instantaneous velocity, cuts through fog/snow Clutter, low angular spatial resolution
3D LiDAR Odometry Iterative Closest Point (ICP) laser scan matching 10–20 Hz Sub-centimeter relative transformation Heavy rain/dust, featureless highways
Stereo Visual Odometry Optical flow feature tracking across camera frames 30–60 Hz Rich semantic texture, lane detection Blinding glare, darkness, low texture

3. The Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) State Estimator

To fuse these disparate telemetry streams into a single optimal velocity vector, autonomous platforms execute an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) or Error-State Kalman Filter (ES-EKF).

Kalman Filter Optimal State Update Formula
\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1} \mathbf{H}_k^T \left( \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{P}_{k|k-1} \mathbf{H}_k^T + \mathbf{R}_k \right)^{-1}
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k} = \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1} + \mathbf{K}_k \left( \mathbf{z}_k - h(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k-1}) \right)

Where $\mathbf{K}_k$ is the Kalman Gain matrix, $\mathbf{P}$ is state covariance, $\mathbf{H}$ is the measurement Jacobian, $\mathbf{R}$ is measurement noise covariance, and $\mathbf{z}_k$ is the incoming sensor observation vector.

4. Velocity Estimation in GPS-Denied Environments (Dead Reckoning)

When an autonomous vehicle enters a 2-kilometer underground road tunnel, satellite GNSS signals are severed instantaneously. How does the vehicle maintain accurate velocity estimation?

  1. Radar Ground Speed Extraction: The 77GHz radar scans static road infrastructure (tunnel walls, overhead lights). By isolating stationary radar reflections and taking the median radial Doppler velocity, the radar provides true vehicle ground speed immune to wheel slip.
  2. LiDAR Scan Registration (ICP / NDT): The LiDAR captures 1,000,000 laser points per second. By mathematically registering consecutive 3D point cloud scans using Normal Distributions Transform (NDT), the vehicle calculates its exact 6-DOF translation and delta velocity ($\Delta \vec{v}$).
  3. IMU Integration with Zero-Velocity Updates (ZUPT): High-grade tactical tactical IMUs integrate acceleration into velocity, bounded by radar and optical flow constraints.

6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why don't autonomous cars just use wheel speed sensors?
Wheel speed sensors only measure tire rotational speed. On icy roads, hydroplaning water puddles, or during heavy braking, wheel speed deviates significantly from true chassis motion (as explained in our CAN-Bus Speed Guide).
How does optical flow measure speed from cameras?
Optical flow algorithms (such as Lucas-Kanade) track the pixel displacement vectors of high-contrast road features across successive 60fps camera frames. Combined with camera height calibration, pixel velocity converts into meters per second.
