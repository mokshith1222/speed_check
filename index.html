FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Speedometer Telematics & GNSS FAQ

Find clear answers regarding satellite reception, mobile browser permissions, tunnel dead reckoning, and vehicle calibration.

Modern smartphones and laptops contain dedicated GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) chips capable of receiving radio signals from GPS (USA), Galileo (EU), GLONASS (Russia), and BeiDou (China) constellations. AeroSpeed interfaces directly with this hardware via the W3C Geolocation API, calculating real-time velocity through continuous positional deltas and carrier-phase Doppler frequency shifts.
Vehicle speedometers are intentionally calibrated by automakers to read higher than true speed to comply with international regulations such as UNECE Regulation 39 and federal motor vehicle safety standards. These laws strictly forbid car speedometers from ever indicating a speed lower than the vehicle's actual speed. Consequently, automakers apply a safety margin (often 3% to 7% higher). AeroSpeed GPS reflects your actual physical road velocity.
GPS signals operate on microwave frequencies that require an unobstructed line of sight to orbiting satellites. Passing through underground tunnels, dense high-rise urban canyons, or multi-level parking garages attenuates signal power, causing temporary dilution of precision (DOP) or latency until satellite lock is re-established.
For maximum HUD clarity: (1) Set your phone display brightness to 100%, (2) place the device on a non-slip dashboard mat directly beneath your line of sight, (3) use the mode during nighttime or overcast conditions. A non-reflective HUD film applied to the windshield can also eliminate double-image refraction caused by dual-pane safety glass.
